Athletics

Jamie Thornton

14-week intervention

The Challenge

Jamie came to me six weeks post-stress fracture — a competitive 400m sprinter with a history of bony injuries, a weak posterior chain, and a competition season starting in 14 weeks. The medical team had cleared him for structured loading. The task was to rebuild his physical capacity from the ground up without triggering a recurrence.

The Approach

Phases one and two focused on rebuilding tendon capacity and single-leg strength through progressive isometric and slow-eccentric loading. Velocity work was introduced in week seven with GymAware to manage neuromuscular output. Sprint work was phased in gradually from week nine, supported by bi-weekly force decks assessments to track limb symmetry.

Football — Academy

Edinburgh FC U18 Squad

12-week pre-season block

The Challenge

The academy coaching staff identified that their U18 squad was consistently out-muscled by opposition in the second half of games. Initial assessment revealed below-average absolute strength scores across all positions, with particular deficits in the posterior chain. The brief: design and implement a pre-season physical preparation block that improved strength without disrupting technical development time.

The Approach

A twice-weekly strength block was integrated into the existing training week structure. Focus: posterior chain development (hip hinge patterns, single-leg work), upper-body pushing and pulling capacity, and repeated sprint ability via resisted acceleration work. Load was auto-regulated using a simple session-RPE system to prevent excessive fatigue during technical sessions.

Rugby Union

Craig Muir

18-month programme

The Challenge

Craig had suffered three hamstring strains in two seasons. He came to me during another rehabilitation period with a clear goal: solve the problem, not just manage it. Initial assessment identified significant bilateral strength asymmetry, a hip-dominant sprint pattern, and significant training load spikes during pre-season as contributing factors.

The Approach

An 18-month programme addressed the asymmetry through targeted unilateral loading and Nordic hamstring eccentric capacity work alongside general strength development. Sprint mechanics were improved through targeted acceleration and top-speed drills. Weekly monitoring via session RPE and jump testing flagged fatigue risk in real time.
