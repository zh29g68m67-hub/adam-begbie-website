About

A Decade In
High Performance

I'm a strength and conditioning coach with close to a decade of experience working across high-performance sport. My work focuses on developing the physical qualities that matter most in field and court-based environments: speed, strength, and change-of-direction ability. I hold a Master's degree in Strength & Conditioning, which underpins the evidence-based approach I bring to coaching.

For me, sport science is most valuable when it can be applied practically. Research and data help guide training design, athlete monitoring, and decision-making, but the priority is always what works in the real world and what helps athletes consistently improve.

A significant part of my work takes place within the BSC Young Boys Academy, where I support players in the final stages of development before professional football. I have learnt a lot working in that environment and seen firsthand what it takes to reach the top.

Alongside football, I work across a range of performance settings including ballet, floorball, and Muay Thai. Although each environment presents different physical and technical demands, the core principles remain the same: structured training, clear intent, and consistent progression.

I believe effective coaching is built on trust. Every athlete responds differently to training, so my role is not only to deliver programmes, but to understand the individual in front of me and adapt the process accordingly. That process starts with communication. Athletes need to understand why they are doing something and how it will impact their sport-specific performance.

I aim to create training environments that are demanding while still supportive. High standards matter, but so does knowing when to adapt. Good coaching is about finding the balance between structure and flexibility, while keeping athletes progressing physically and mentally over time.

The coach–athlete relationship sits at the centre of my practice. In many environments, the S&C coach is often the first and last point of contact in an athlete's day, and I take that responsibility seriously. Physical preparation is important, but the environment surrounding it matters just as much. Athletes perform best when there is trust, accountability, and honest communication. The same applies to the coaching team around the athlete. Trust and honest communication between staff create the best environment for long-term development and performance.

I see coaching as a long-term process rather than a short-term intervention. Sustainable progress comes from consistency built over time. My role is to provide structure and support while helping athletes take ownership of their development.
