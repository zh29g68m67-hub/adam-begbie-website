About

Science Meets
The Coaching Floor

Adam is a strength and conditioning coach whose work sits at the point where research and practice genuinely meet — not as separate disciplines, but as one way of thinking. His Master's degree in Strength & Conditioning isn't a credential bolted onto a coaching career; it's the lens through which every session, programme, and decision is made.

A significant part of his work takes place within the BSC Young Boys Academy, where he supports the physical development of players in the final stage before professional football. At this level, the margins are small and the demands are specific — his focus is on the qualities that determine whether a player can survive and compete in the professional game: speed, acceleration, and change-of-direction ability. These aren't general athletic qualities to be ticked off; they're the mechanical and neuromuscular outputs that the science of football demands, developed with precision and purpose.

As a person, Adam places real value on open exchange — with athletes, with fellow coaches, and with the research community. He takes time to understand the people he works with beyond their performance data, because he believes that knowing an athlete as a person is part of the methodology, not a distraction from it. That same curiosity shapes how he continues to develop within the field.

Research

Areas of
Focus

01

Force-Velocity Profiling

Individualised assessment of the force-velocity mechanical spectrum to inform targeted power development programming and monitor adaptation over time.

02

Velocity-Based Training

Using intent-to-move and real-time velocity feedback to autoregulate training load, preserve neuromuscular quality, and detect fatigue during training blocks.

03

Youth Athlete Development

Long-term physical development in academy-aged athletes — navigating the PHV period, relative age effects, and the balance between sport-specific and general athletic capacity.
